Student rocketry team · HALO, a VTVL hopper

Vice President, Sep 2024 – Jun 2026, UC Santa Barbara

HALO: a hopper that takes off, hovers, and lands on its own legs.

HALO is the team's vertical take-off, vertical landing vehicle: a ~3 kN nitrous oxide / isopropanol engine on a gimbal, four aluminum rails carrying the tanks and avionics, cold-gas roll thrusters and splayed landing legs, built for a stable hover and a ~100 m hop. As vice president I owned integration: structures, propulsion, actuators, sensors, custom PCBs, power electronics and controls had to fit, survive and work together.

  • Ran FEA and structural optimization to trade mass against stiffness, strength and factor of safety.
  • Designed for manufacturing across CNC machining, additive, sheet metal and composites.
  • Organized and led design reviews with faculty and industry sponsors.

Senior capstone, in progress

Team iME8, 2026 – 2027, sponsored by Genentech

An xArm 6 that scoops, taps and stops on target.

For my senior capstone, sponsored by Genentech, my team is building a closed-loop robotic workcell in which an xArm 6 arm scoops pharmaceutical powders with a spatula and dispenses them onto an analytical balance to targets from 0.1 to 100 g at 0.01 g resolution.

  • I researched and wrote the team's setup guides for running ROS 2 Humble in a Docker container and bringing up the xArm 6 in simulation with the manufacturer's MoveIt packages, the base every scale, camera and arm node builds on.
  • The powders behave very differently: microcrystalline cellulose flows freely, magnesium stearate is hydrophobic and dusty, and colloidal silicon dioxide is ultra-light, cohesive and static-prone.
  • Our design pairs coarse, collision-free motion planning with a fine closed-loop controller that taps harder when far from the target, gently near it, and stops just early.

Junior capstone

Autonomous Shopping Cart

Team Zero, Apr – Jun 2026, UC Santa Barbara

A cart that picks one shopper out of a crowd and follows, with all of the intelligence on board.

Team Zero's junior capstone. The hardware is modest, a Raspberry Pi 5, an AI camera and an ESP32, so the project was really the software: on-device person detection and re-identification, a tracking lock that survives occlusion, and self-tuning motion control, all at 30 fps with no cloud.

  • Perception: YOLO11n runs on the IMX500 camera's own neural processor, ByteTrack tracks everyone in view, and a regression-trained color-fingerprint matcher re-identifies the shopper after occlusions. Everyone else becomes an obstacle.
  • Controls: continuous damped-proportional distance and steering laws with deadband, hysteresis and slew limits, a kick-start that beats floor friction, and a nine-mode state machine for follow, blocked, search and return-home.
  • Learning: the cart learns its own linear and angular momentum from every stop and turn and brakes early to land on target, with no calibration session. Return-home runs on dead-reckoned odometry with wheel-slip rejection.
