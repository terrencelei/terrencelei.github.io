Senior capstone, in progress

Robotic powder dispensing for Genentech

Team iME8, 2026 – 2027, sponsored by Genentech

An xArm 6 that scoops, taps and stops on target.

For my senior capstone, sponsored by Genentech, my team is building a closed-loop robotic workcell in which an xArm 6 arm scoops pharmaceutical powders with a spatula and dispenses them onto an analytical balance to targets from 0.1 to 100 g at 0.01 g resolution.

  • I researched and wrote the team's setup guides for running ROS 2 Humble in a Docker container and bringing up the xArm 6 in simulation with the manufacturer's MoveIt packages, the base every scale, camera and arm node builds on.
  • The powders behave very differently: microcrystalline cellulose flows freely, magnesium stearate is hydrophobic and dusty, and colloidal silicon dioxide is ultra-light, cohesive and static-prone.
  • Our design pairs coarse, collision-free motion planning with a fine closed-loop controller that taps harder when far from the target, gently near it, and stops just early.
  • ROS 2 Humble
  • Docker
  • MoveIt
  • xArm 6
  • Closed-loop control

About me

Mechanical engineering with a statistics habit.

I'm a mechanical engineering student at UC Santa Barbara, specializing in dynamic systems, control and robotics, with a second degree in statistics and data science. I like the point where a mechanism meets the data that proves it works.

That thread runs through my work. As vice president of the Gaucho Rocket Project I helped integrate structures, propulsion, avionics and controls into flight hardware, and used FEA to trade mass against strength. At Northrop Grumman I used regression and classification to trace assembly failures to their root causes and released GD&T drawings for legacy flight components. At Applied Materials I worked on a patent-pending actuator that gets two distinct motions from one motor.

Right now I'm automating a materials-testing bench with a robot arm and cameras in the Hawkes Lab, and my Genentech-sponsored capstone team is building a closed-loop robotic powder-dispensing workcell. I'm as comfortable at the mill, the 3D printer or the soldering iron as I am in SolidWorks, Python or MATLAB.

I'm based in Campbell, California, and I'm looking for new-grad mechanical engineering and robotics roles starting June 2027.
